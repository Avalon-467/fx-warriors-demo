Behind the forecast.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Real reference data.
A deliberately transparent model.

FX Warriors combines published daily exchange rates with a simple historical model and user-controlled news scenarios. It demonstrates an explainable FX application. It is not a trained multimodal AI predictor or a trading system.

01 / Data

Daily ECB reference rates are loaded from a bundled, dated snapshot. Refresh requests a newer series from the Frankfurter API; if that fails, the snapshot remains available and labelled.

All supported currency pairs are calculated from the same daily EUR reference table. Weekends and days without published observations are omitted.

02 / Projection

The model uses log returns. Its daily drift is 25% of the mean of the latest 20 returns; volatility is the sample standard deviation of up to 60 returns.

The central path compounds this reduced drift. The 80% interval uses a normal-return approximation and grows with the square root of the horizon. Its coverage is not guaranteed or calibrated.

central(h) = rate × exp(drift × h)
range(h) = central(h) × exp(±1.282 × σ × √h)

03 / News scenarios

The headline analyser matches a small set of economic keywords and suggests an editable scenario. Quoted text is not sent to an external AI service.

A selected impact changes the terminal projected rate by the chosen percentage and is applied gradually across the horizon. This is an assumption, not an estimated causal effect. Holiday dates in the projection are approximate weekdays.

04 / Privacy & control

Budget scenarios are stored in your browser's local storage only when you save them. You can delete them individually or clear them all.

There are no accounts, analytics scripts, trade execution, or backend storage of entered amounts and headlines. Hosting and rate providers still receive ordinary web request metadata.

FROM DEMO TO RESEARCH

The next questions to test.

Does timestamped news add value beyond prices? Can forecast intervals be calibrated out of sample? Do explanations improve users' understanding? A production study would need longer datasets, point-in-time economic releases, a trained model, and a separate holdout evaluation.
